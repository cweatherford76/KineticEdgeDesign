# Kinetic Edge Design — Business Setup Checklist

Working list for getting the business legally, financially, and publicly
ready to take on paid client work. Check items off as you go
(`- [ ]` → `- [x]`) and commit the changes so progress is tracked over time.

## 1. LLC Formation

- [ ] Confirm state of formation (usually the state you live/work in)
- [ ] Confirm the business name is available in that state ("Kinetic Edge Design LLC" or similar)
- [ ] Decide on a registered agent (yourself, if your state allows it, or a registered agent service)
- [ ] File **Articles of Organization** with the state's business filing office
  - [ ] Pay the state filing fee
  - [ ] Save the confirmed/stamped copy once approved
- [ ] Draft and sign an **Operating Agreement**
  - [ ] Ownership structure (single-member, in your case)
  - [ ] How the business can be dissolved or ownership transferred
  - [ ] Even as a single-member LLC, this document matters. It's what banks
        and courts look at to confirm the LLC is being run as a real
        separate entity, not just a personal side project
- [ ] Apply for an **EIN** from the IRS (free, done online in minutes, needed before the bank account)
- [ ] Check whether your state/city requires a general business license or permit
- [ ] Check whether your state requires an annual report/renewal filing, and note the due date somewhere you'll actually see it

## 2. Banking & Money

- [ ] Open a business bank account (needs the EIN + Articles of Organization)
- [ ] Set up a way to invoice and get paid (e.g., Stripe Invoicing, PayPal Business)
- [ ] Set up basic bookkeeping (e.g., Wave, QuickBooks, or even a dedicated spreadsheet to start)
- [ ] Decide on deposit/final-payment split for website design projects (flagged as still-undecided in the site's README)
- [ ] Get a quote for Errors & Omissions (E&O) and General Liability insurance

## 3. Contracts & Service Agreements

- [ ] Draft a standard service agreement / contract template covering:
  - [ ] Scope of work per package (what's included, what isn't)
  - [ ] Hour ranges for Intermediate, Advanced, and Custom Applications work, with an overage clause for anything beyond the estimate
  - [ ] Payment terms and what happens on late payment
  - [ ] Revision limits per package
  - [ ] Hosting contract terms (the 12-month commitment tied to the 50% design discount)
  - [ ] Ownership/IP terms (who owns the final site/code)
  - [ ] Cancellation/termination terms
- [ ] Have a lawyer (or a service like Rocket Lawyer / a local small-business attorney) review the template once drafted, even if you write the first draft yourself

## 4. Online Presence & Marketing

- [ ] Set up a **Google Business Profile** (highest priority: this is what drives local search visibility and reviews)
- [ ] Create a **Facebook Business Page**, linked from the site footer
- [ ] Decide if Instagram is worth it. Only start one if you'll actually post regularly (client work screenshots, progress shots); a dead page hurts more than no page
- [ ] Add social links to the site once created
- [ ] Consider basic, privacy-friendly site analytics (e.g., Plausible or GA4) so you can see what's actually driving traffic once marketing starts
